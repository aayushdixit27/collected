Collected

Proof of the weight. The scale ticket from the transfer station, tied to the customer's container, on one page with the pickup photo and the math.

The problem

The record exists and cannot be tied to the customer.

A roll-off hauler dumps at someone else's transfer station. The scale house prints a paper ticket with gross, tare and net on it, and no container and no customer. Three weeks later an overage charge lands. The customer asks for proof, gets that ticket, and cannot tell it was their load.

"The weight ticket provided did not have any identifying information that it belonged to our reserved container. I cannot confirm it was actually our ticket." — Trustpilot review, August 2026

The people

Dale runs four roll-off trucks. He does not own a scale, so his drivers dump across town and come back with a paper ticket. His flat rate includes one ton; anything over is billed at $95 a ton, from that paper, when it finally reaches the office.

Renée is a contractor who rents twenty of Dale's boxes a year. On a Tuesday she gets an invoice for a pull from three weeks ago: $158.65 over. She asks for proof. Dale sends the ticket. It has a weight and a time and nothing that says it was her box. She does not think Dale is a crook. She just cannot check, and she has another hauler's number.

Without Collected. When Renée asks for proof, the ticket Dale has is a photo of paper with a weight, a time and maybe a truck number on it. Nothing on it says Renée, her site, or her box. He can send it; she can't check it.

With Collected. Three things land on one record: the pickup (container ID, address, time, and where the phone was); the ticket (the photo from the scale house and the net weight, minutes later); the charge (net minus the included ton, times the rate). When the overage goes out, it goes with a link:

Pickup 1428 Mission College Blvd · RO-20-114 · Aug 12, 6:36 AM Ticket Zanker Road Transfer Station · 7:41 AM (+64 min) Gross 19,860 lb Tare 14,520 lb Net 5,340 lb Charge 5,340 − 2,000 included = 3,340 lb over 3,340 ÷ 2,000 × $95.00/ton = $158.65

Renée sees the box at her address, the ticket 64 minutes later, the math, and $158.65. She can check it. That is the whole product. (Composite; names invented.)

Who gets what

DriverTwo photos inside the work he already does, the box and the ticket. One photo, one number. Shows he did the job.
The haulerBills the overage with proof attached, the day the ticket is printed. Fewer "is that my ticket?" calls, fewer refunds.
The customerCan check the charge in ten seconds. Pays what she owes, keeps the hauler she trusts.

How it is measured

Share of billed overages that go out with a ticket tied to the container. Zero if the ticket was never photographed; zero if it was photographed and sits in a text thread. Inputs: ticket step under 20 seconds, tickets tied per pull, the customer's page found in under 30 seconds from an address and a date.

In the hauler's terms: fewer ticket-mismatch complaints and fewer refund dollars, month over month. An example, not a measurement: a hauler with 100 disputed tickets a month who refunds half at $75 each is giving back $3,750 a month. That number, before and after, is the one to watch.

Three product decisions

  • The ticket, not the pickup photo. The platform's own driver app already photographs the pickup and ties it to the job. What it does not hand the customer is the third-party ticket with the container and the math. One competitor shows ticket images; the difference here is the container link and the arithmetic, at the cost of one photo and one number.
  • One record, two photos, one place. The stop is the object. Pickup and ticket are two slots on it, filled in the moment or later, through the same two taps. Attaching the ticket in a separate flow was tried on 17 September and failed in front of a user.
  • Never trade capture speed. Pickup under ten seconds; the ticket is one photo and one number. A field that improves the record but slows the driver loses.

Next

  1. Read the ticket. Pull gross, tare, net and the facility off the photo, and name the transfer station from the GPS fix; the driver confirms one number instead of typing it.
  2. The charge carries the page. Every overage line on the invoice gets the link automatically; an overage with no ticket tied is held.
  3. Could-not-service notice. The same record, sent the same day a box is blocked, overfilled or not out.

How it was built, and what was not checked

Spec before code. Three coding agents built the ticket feature in parallel from one contract, each owning separate files, each reviewed independently; a fresh review of the merged result found five defects before deploy. Their briefs and findings live in a shared Senso folder, the context layer the agents and I both read and write, so a new session picks up the state without a transcript.

Not checked: no hauler has been spoken to. A first-time user on his own phone recorded a pull in 41 seconds and tied the ticket in 22, against a 30-second budget. Records are files on Vercel Blob, written once; the store lost records on 17 September under two devices writing at once and was rebuilt that afternoon. A real product would keep records in Postgres and photos in Blob.
