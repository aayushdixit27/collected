Collected

The weight ticket, tied to your container. A two-hour take-home for a waste-hauling platform, and what it turned into.

The problem

The record exists and cannot be tied to the customer.

A roll-off hauler dumps at someone else's transfer station. The scale house prints a paper ticket — gross, tare, net — with no container and no customer on it. Three weeks later an overage charge lands. The customer asks for proof, gets that ticket, and cannot tell it was their load.

"The weight ticket provided did not have any identifying information that it belonged to our reserved container. I cannot confirm it was actually our ticket." — a customer, public review, August 2026

What it does

The stop is the record. The driver takes the pickup photo and, at the scale, the ticket photo plus one number — the net weight. The office finds any pull weeks later by address and date. The customer gets a page they can check:

Pickup 1428 Mission College Blvd · RO-20-114 · Aug 12, 6:36 AM Ticket Zanker Road Transfer Station · 7:41 AM (+64 min) Gross 19,860 lb Tare 14,520 lb Net 5,340 lb Charge 5,340 − 2,000 included = 3,340 lb over 3,340 ÷ 2,000 × $95.00/ton = $158.65

Three product decisions

  • Retrieval, not capture. The platform's own driver app already photographs the pickup. The paper ticket from a third-party scale, tied to the container and shown to the customer with the arithmetic, is the part nobody hands over.
  • Never trade capture speed. Pickup under ten seconds; the ticket is one photo and one number. A field that improves the record but slows the driver loses.
  • Say what was not checked. No hauler has been spoken to. Real phone captures ran 22–40 s against the 10 s target. Storage was redesigned on 17 Sep after it lost records under two devices writing at once.

How it was built

Spec before code. Three parallel coding agents, each owning disjoint files, built the ticket feature from one contract in about an hour; each agent's work went through an independent reviewer, and a fresh-context review of the merged result found five defects before deploy. Their briefs, findings and reviews live in a shared Senso folder — the context layer the agents and I read and write, so a new session picks up the state without a transcript. The record store is Vercel Blob with write-once files; a real product would put records in Postgres and keep Blob for photos.
