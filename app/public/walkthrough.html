01The problem

The record exists and cannot be tied to the customer.

A roll-off hauler who does not own a scale dumps at a transfer station. The scale house prints a paper ticket with gross, tare and net on it, and nothing that says whose container it came from. Weeks later an overage charge arrives with that ticket. The customer cannot tell it was their load.

"The weight ticket provided did not have any identifying information that it belonged to our reserved container. I cannot confirm it was actually our ticket."Trustpilot, verified review, 13 August 2026. A BBB complaint in June describes the same thing and ended in a company-reported $1,989 refund.

Two complaints show the problem exists. Nobody has counted how often, and no hauler has been asked.

02Without, with

Dale runs four roll-off trucks and dumps across town. Renée is a contractor who rents twenty of his boxes a year. On a Tuesday she gets an invoice for a pull from three weeks ago, $158.65 over, and asks for proof.

Without Collected, Dale sends the ticket: a photo of paper with a weight and a time on it. Nothing says Renée, her site, or her box. She does not think he is a crook; she cannot check, and she has another hauler's number.

With Collected, three things sit on one record: the pickup (container, address, time, where the phone was), the ticket (the photo from the scale house and the net weight, an hour later), and the charge. Renée gets a link.

03How it is measured

North Star · an input, not an outcome

Share of billed overages that go out with a ticket tied to the container.

It scores zero when the ticket was never photographed, and zero when it was photographed and sits in a text thread. Both failures are the same failure to the customer. Three inputs move it:

InputTargetWhy it moves the number
Ticket step time< 20 sThe adoption lever, twice per pull
Tickets tied per pull%Adoption, measured
Time to the customer's page< 30 sFrom an address and a date; the office half

Reported beside it, not steered: disputes closed with evidence and refunds avoided, by count and by dollars.

The office does not compute the North Star yet. Today it shows pickup coverage (91 % of scheduled stops on recorded days). The tickets-tied strip is the first build after submission.

04Three decisions, and what each traded

The ticket, not the pickup photo.TrashLab's driver app, per its product page, photographs the pickup and ties it to the job. What it does not hand the customer is the third-party ticket with the container and the math. Docket, per its support pages, shows customers a ticket image; the difference here is the container link and the arithmetic.
The first version was the pickup record. Checking it against TrashLab's driver app showed they already capture the pickup and tie it to the job, so the build moved to the piece their customers were complaining about: the ticket.
One record, two photos, one screen.The stop is the object; pickup and ticket are two slots on it, filled now or later, through the same taps.
Traded: a separate "attach ticket" flow, tried first; a first-time user could not find it, so it was folded into the one screen.
Never trade capture speed.Pickup under ten seconds; the ticket is one photo and one number. A field that improves the record but slows the driver loses.
Traded: gross, tare and facility are optional, so the record can be thinner than the ticket.

06Next

  • Read the ticket. Gross, tare, net and facility off the photo; the transfer station from the GPS fix. The driver confirms one number instead of typing it. Cuts the ticket step.
  • The charge carries the page. Every overage line on the invoice gets the link; an overage with no ticket tied is held. Moves the North Star.
  • Could-not-service notice. The same record, sent the day a box is blocked, overfilled or not out. The other charge customers dispute.

07How it was built

Spec before code: record model, API, acceptance criteria, non-goals. Three coding agents built the ticket feature in parallel from that one contract, each owning separate files, each reviewed independently; a fresh review of the merged result found five defects before deploy, one of them a stored XSS in the facility field. Briefs and findings live in a shared knowledge base (Senso) that the agents and I both read and write, so a new session picks up the state without a transcript.

A live demo on 17 September exposed a storage race: five records were lost when two devices wrote at once. The cause came out of the file history in twenty minutes; the store was rebuilt that afternoon as write-once files. A real product keeps records in Postgres and photos in Blob.

08Not checked

  • No hauler has been spoken to. Day one is a hauler on the phone: where do your drivers dump, and what does the ticket look like?
  • Whether TrashLab's "overweight loads" workflow already covers a ticket photo. First question for John.
  • Whether roll-off haulers in TrashLab's base dump at third-party scales at all.
  • Whether Docket's customer-visible ticket images already settle this for its users.
  • Android; a physical print of the customer's page; a driver, not a first-time user, on the ticket step.
